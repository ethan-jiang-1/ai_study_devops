## Repository Overview

This is a research repository focused on AI's impact on various development practices and methodologies. The repository contains comprehensive research reports and analyses across multiple domains:

- **DevOps**: Traditional DevOps practices vs AI-enhanced DevOps
- **Project Management & Business Analysis (PMBA)**: AI transformation in project management
- **Research & Development (R&D)**: AI's impact on R&D processes
- **Test-Driven Development (TDD)**: Classic TDD vs AI-enhanced TDD approaches
- **Test Assets**: AI integration in testing frameworks and asset management

## Repository Structure

The repository is organized into domain-specific exploration directories:

- `EXPLORE_DEVOPS/`: DevOps research and reports
- `EXPLORE_PMBA/`: Project Management & Business Analysis research
- `EXPLORE_RND/`: Research & Development impact analysis
- `EXPLORE_TDD/`: Test-Driven Development research
- `EXPLORE_TestAsset/`: Test asset management research

Each exploration directory follows a consistent pattern:
- `research_dr*/`: Raw research data from various AI models
- `result/`: Final reports and structured outputs
- `result_ppt/`: Presentation-ready slide formats
- `_extra/`: Additional supporting materials

## File Types and Formats

This repository primarily contains:
- Markdown documentation (`.md` files)
- PDF reports (`.pdf` files)
- Research data organized by AI model source (deepseek, gemini, openai, etc.)

## Working with This Repository

Since this is a documentation and research repository without traditional development toolchains:

- No package.json, Makefile, or build scripts are present
- No linting or testing commands are configured
- Content is primarily static documentation that can be read and edited directly

When working with files in this repository:
- Maintain the existing directory structure and naming conventions
- Follow the established pattern of organizing research by AI model source
- Keep reports in both markdown and PDF formats where applicable
- Preserve the separation between raw research data and processed results
